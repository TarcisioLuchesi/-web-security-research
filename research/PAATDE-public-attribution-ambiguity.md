# PAATDE — Public Attribution Ambiguity Through Deliberate Exposure

**Proposed by:** Tarcísio Luchesi (Poystick)  
**Date:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** Conceptual, unvalidated hypothesis; not an official MITRE ATT&CK technique  
**Topics:** OPSEC, OSINT, public attribution, reverse psychology, counterintelligence, social perception

## Abstract

PAATDE (Public Attribution Ambiguity Through Deliberate Exposure) is a proposed name for a hypothesis about how deliberate and truthful public disclosure of a security researcher's legitimate collaboration with a government or other institution could affect how external observers infer affiliation, motivation, and capability. Public acknowledgments, including a name and photograph in news or institutional publications, may produce inaccurate assumptions about official membership or involvement in advanced persistent threat (APT) activity.

It is **not established** that this constitutes reverse psychology, provides effective operational security, or reliably alters attribution. Public exposure generally increases traceability.

## Hypothetical scenario

1. An independent cybersecurity researcher makes a legitimate, authorized contribution to a public institution or government project.
2. With permission, the institution publicly acknowledges that contribution, perhaps displaying the researcher's name and photo in an official article or news report.
3. Observers may interpret the documented contribution as evidence of a formal relationship, although a collaboration does not establish employment, military affiliation, or APT membership.
4. Analysts should distinguish verified facts from speculative connections.

## Analytical concepts

- **Attribution ambiguity:** uncertainty about the nature or extent of an individual's institutional association.
- **Public exposure:** voluntary publication of identity and collaboration details, subject to permission and truthful representation.
- **Reverse psychology:** a proposed psychological interpretation, which would require additional behavioral evidence; mere publicity does not demonstrate it.
- **OSINT correlation:** publication of identifiers can increase an observer's ability to correlate accounts, relationships, timelines, and public records.

## Falsifiable research questions

- Does a verified public acknowledgment measurably increase the rate at which neutral observers infer formal membership rather than independent collaboration?
- Can an evidence-based attribution protocol reduce such false inferences?
- Does identity exposure demonstrably increase traceability and personal security risks?
- Is reverse psychology actually involved, or are ordinary cognitive biases and ambiguity sufficient explanations?

Experiments should use consenting participants, simulated institutions, and fictional scenarios. No impersonation, fabricated reporting, unauthorized access, or misleading claims of government or APT affiliation are needed.

## Security implications and limitations

Publicly releasing a name or photo can undermine OPSEC by enabling identity correlation, profiling, and harassment. This is therefore **not a recommended OPSEC defense**. Even accurate press coverage cannot establish that an individual belongs to a state-sponsored operation. Advanced Persistent Threat is an analytic designation that requires reliable technical and intelligence evidence, not merely apparent institutional association.

**Conclusion:** PAATDE is a proposed, untested research label for studying public attribution and perception. It must not be presented as an independently validated technique, a MITRE ATT&CK classification, or a proven attribution-evasion method.
